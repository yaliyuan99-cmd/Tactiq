Taking part in the research

Tactiq is being designed with blind and low-vision people, not for them. This page explains what taking part involves, and the commitments every participant can hold us to.

Study status

Recruitment has not opened yet. The interview study is being prepared for review-board approval at the researcher's school, following the international student science fair (ISEF) human-participant rules. No participant will be approached before that approval is granted, in writing.

When recruitment opens, it will run through blindness organisations, which will circulate the invitation to their communities. We do not ask organisations for member lists, and we never contact anyone directly out of the blue.

What the study is: one conversation of about 45 to 60 minutes about how you really use your smartphone day to day, in person in Sydney or by phone or video from anywhere in Australia. No preparation is needed, and there are no right or wrong answers. Honest criticism of the Tactiq concept is the most valuable contribution you can make.

Our commitments to participants

  • Your time is paid. A $50 gift card per interview, plus travel costs for in-person sessions. You keep it even if you stop early.
  • Every document comes in your format. Standard print, large print, screen-reader-friendly electronic text, and audio as standard; braille on request.
  • Consent on your terms. Written or recorded verbal consent count equally. You can skip any question, stop at any time, and withdraw within four weeks of a session, with your data deleted, no reason needed.
  • Nothing fires without review. Ethics approval comes before any recruitment; a supervising adult and, where the host organisation requires it, their staff member are present at all in-person sessions, which take place at organisation premises, the school, or public venues.
  • Your name never appears. Interviews are de-identified immediately; anonymous quotes are used only with your permission; recordings live on a password-protected device and are deleted at the end of the project.
  • You hear the results. Every participant who wants one receives a plain-language summary of the findings, in their preferred format, and so does every organisation that helped.
  • No claims without evidence. Anything we say publicly about what blind users want must be traceable to what participants actually said, and performance figures stay labelled as pre-registered targets until tests pass.
  • No sales pitch. Taking part is not a waitlist and creates no obligation. The research exists to find out whether this idea deserves to be built at all.

The full participant information sheet and consent forms exist now and will be published on this page when the study opens. Until then they are available on request, in any of the formats above, through the researcher's school.

For organisations

If you work with the blind and low-vision community and are considering circulating this study, here is what you will receive before being asked to share anything: the written review-board approval, the participant information sheet in all four formats, the recruitment notice, and the safeguarding arrangements for sessions. Participants come to us through you; we never ask for contact lists. After the study, you receive the aggregate findings for your community.

The two research papers behind the project are described on the research page, and full texts are available on request.
